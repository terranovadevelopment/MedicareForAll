Understanding the System

Universal Healthcare Is Not Government-Run Healthcare

Understanding the difference between public insurance and privately delivered medical care.

Universal healthcare means that everyone has access to an established system of healthcare coverage. It does not, by itself, specify who owns hospitals or employs physicians. Different countries use different combinations of public and private insurance and care delivery.

Under a single-payer Medicare for All proposal, a public program would finance covered care, while many physicians, dentists, clinics, and hospitals could continue operating privately. Patients would receive care from healthcare professionals, not from government employees simply because the insurance is publicly financed.

That distinction does not mean government would have no role in healthcare decisions. A national payer would establish coverage rules, reimbursement arrangements, and accountability standards. These rules would need to be transparent and designed to preserve access and appropriate clinical judgment.

Debating Medicare for All is easier when we separate two questions: who pays for care, and who provides it. They are related, but they are not the same.
