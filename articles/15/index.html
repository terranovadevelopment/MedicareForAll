Article 15

If America Is the Greatest Country, Let’s Prove It

Let’s build a healthcare system worthy of America’s ideals—one that puts We the People first and leaves no one last.

Download illustrated Word article

Build a healthcare system that puts “We the People” first—and leaves no one last.

The challenge is not merely to call America great, but to build institutions worthy of that promise.

Greatness Is Something We Do

Americans love to say that ours is the greatest country in the world. That pride can inspire us. But greatness is not established by repeating a slogan. It is demonstrated by what we accomplish together—and by how we treat people when they are sick, vulnerable, or afraid.

If we have the talent to pioneer new medicines, build remarkable hospitals, and develop technologies that transform lives, surely we have the ability to design a healthcare system in which every person can receive needed care without financial ruin.

A Healthcare System Worthy of Our Ideals

Imagine an America where a mother does not delay a child’s appointment because of the cost; where a retiree does not have to choose between prescriptions and groceries; where a worker can change jobs or start a business without fearing the loss of health coverage. Imagine doctors and nurses spending less time fighting insurance paperwork and more time caring for patients.

That should be the standard we aim for—not merely coverage on paper, but practical access to timely, high-quality care. Medicare for All offers one path toward that goal: a publicly financed system designed to cover everyone and reduce financial barriers at the point of care.

We Already Have Enormous Resources

The United States spends extraordinary sums on healthcare. Yet people still face medical debt, complicated bills, uneven access, and uncertainty about what their insurance will pay. The central question is not whether healthcare requires money. It does. The question is whether we are organizing those resources as effectively and fairly as we could.

A universal system would not be free to operate, and its design would require difficult choices about taxes, provider payment, transition costs, and the availability of services. But the costs of the present system are real too: premiums, deductibles, copayments, administrative burdens, and care delayed until illness becomes more severe.

Putting “We the People” First

The Constitution begins with “We the People.” Those words are a reminder that government exists to serve the public. Healthcare reform should be judged by that principle: Does it help people live healthier lives? Does it protect families from catastrophic costs? Does it support medical professionals in delivering good care? Does it use public and private resources responsibly?

This is not a call to turn Americans against one another. Patients, employers, physicians, nurses, taxpayers, and communities all have a stake in a system that works. We can disagree about policy details while agreeing that no one should be abandoned because they become ill.

A Challenge to the Nation

Let us stop measuring greatness only by what we say about ourselves. Let us measure it by what we build. Let us demand serious proposals, honest accounting, and a willingness to improve ideas rather than dismiss them by political label.

We should aspire to the greatest healthcare system ever seen: one that encourages medical excellence, supports innovation, respects caregivers, controls unnecessary costs, and makes care accessible to every person. That is an ambitious goal. America has never been at its best when it was afraid of ambitious goals.

If America is the greatest country in the world, then let’s prove it.

Let’s build a healthcare system that puts We the People first—and leaves no one last.

By Willis Butts | Retired systems engineer and Medicare for All advocate
