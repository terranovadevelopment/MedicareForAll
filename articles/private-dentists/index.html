Dental Care

What Medicare for All Could Mean for Private Dentists

A closer look at patient access, reimbursement, independence, and the economics of a dental practice.

A Medicare for All proposal that includes comprehensive dental benefits could change the business model of a private dental office without requiring government ownership of the practice. Dentists could continue to own their offices, employ their teams, and care for patients. The biggest change would be how covered services are financed.

Today, dental offices navigate many insurance plans with different annual limits, exclusions, and payment rules. A standardized national system could reduce some of that administrative work. Broader coverage might also help patients seek preventive care and necessary treatment instead of postponing visits because of cost.

The crucial question is reimbursement. Dental practices have substantial expenses for employees, equipment, laboratories, facilities, and professional liability. If public payment rates adequately reflect those costs, wider coverage and simpler billing could benefit practices. If rates are too low, private dentists could face financial strain.

A responsible transition would involve dentists in setting payment rules, protecting professional judgment, and ensuring that patients can find participating providers. Universal coverage is meaningful only when it is accompanied by genuine access to care.
