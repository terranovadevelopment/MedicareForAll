A National Plan to Relieve Medical Debt

Exploring a public refinancing approach that could free families from existing medical bills.

Medical debt can follow families long after treatment ends. It can affect credit, housing, savings, and decisions about future care. Any transition to universal healthcare should consider not only how to prevent new medical debt but also what to do about the obligations people already carry.

One proposal is for the federal government to acquire or refinance eligible medical debts and relieve patients of their payment obligations. The government could then retire the resulting public obligations over time through an established budget schedule. The idea is to prevent families from remaining trapped by old bills while making the financial transition transparent.

The details would matter greatly. Policymakers would need to verify legitimate debts, prevent inflated claims, avoid rewarding abusive collection practices, and determine fair purchase prices. Public financing would still involve real fiscal costs, even if the government chose a long repayment horizon.

The broader objective is simple: a healthcare reform should make it easier for people to recover financially, not merely change the way future bills are paid.
